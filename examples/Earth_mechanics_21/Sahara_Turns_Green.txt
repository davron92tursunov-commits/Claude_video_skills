TITLE
The Sahara Was a Jungle 🌿 What If It Turned Green Again?

DESCRIPTION
What if the Sahara turned green again? It has happened before.
The Sahara covers about nine million square kilometers, roughly the size of the United States, and most of it gets less than one hundred millimeters of rain a year.
Until about six thousand years ago it was grassland with lakes, hippos and rivers. Lake Megachad may have covered around four hundred thousand square kilometers.
The cause was a slow wobble of Earth's axis, which repeats about every twenty-three thousand years and pushes the African monsoon north.
Today the dust matters too: about twenty-seven million tons of Saharan dust cross the Atlantic to the Amazon every year and fertilize its soil.
The next natural green phase is estimated to be around ten thousand years away.
The Megachad size and the next green phase date are estimates.
Visuals are simplified and illustrative.
Map data: Natural Earth (public domain). Dust data: NASA CALIPSO (Yu et al. 2015). 3D plants: Kenney Nature Kit (CC0).

FACT CHECK
- Sahara area ~9.2 million km2 (about 9.2-9.4 depending on source). US 9.8 million km2, so "almost the size". OK.
- Rain under 100 mm/year: hyperarid core receives far less; most of the Sahara under 100 mm. OK.
- African Humid Period ended about 5,500-6,000 years ago. "Six thousand years ago" OK. Hippo fossils at Gobero (Niger) and Lake Turkana. OK.
- Lake Megachad ~360,000-400,000 km2 at its peak. ESTIMATE.
- Cause: orbital precession ~23,000 years shifts the monsoon north (Kutzbach and others). OK.
- Dust: about 182 million tons leave the Sahara per year, about 27.7 million tons reach the Amazon (NASA CALIPSO, Yu et al. 2015). OK, rounded.
- "Could starve the rainforest": scientific hypothesis, simplified. ESTIMATE.
- Next green phase ~10,000 years: model-based ESTIMATE (precession cycle).
