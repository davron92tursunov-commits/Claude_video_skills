VOICE: Fola

=== STYLE INSTRUCTIONS ===
Read this as a documentary narrator: warm, curious, natural American English.
Pace: 135 to 140 words per minute. Pause after each sentence.
Longer pause before "Right now?", before "So, what if", and before "Don't worry."
"Right now?" low and serious. The "So, what if" question rises at the end.
Stress every number: nine million, one hundred, four hundred thousand, six thousand, twenty-three thousand, twenty-seven million, ten thousand.
"Then comes the scary part." slower and darker.
From "Don't worry." to the end: calm and reassuring.

=== TEXT ===
The Sahara is a jungle.

Right now?

Not yet.

So, what if the Sahara turned green?

Nine million square kilometers of sand.

Almost the size of the United States.

Today it gets less than one hundred millimeters of rain a year.

Now the rain comes back.

Grass first. Then trees. Then lakes.

One lake grows to four hundred thousand square kilometers.

Hippos swim where dunes stand.

This isn't fantasy.

Six thousand years ago, it was real.

A slow wobble of Earth's axis did it.

Every twenty-three thousand years, it pulls the rain north.

Then comes the scary part.

The dust stops.

Twenty-seven million tons of it feed the Amazon every year.

A green Sahara could starve the rainforest.

Don't worry.

The next green phase is about ten thousand years away.

Follow to see how Earth really works.
