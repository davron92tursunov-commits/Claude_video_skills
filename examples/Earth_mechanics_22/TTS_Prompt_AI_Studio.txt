=== STYLE INSTRUCTIONS ===
Read this as a documentary narrator: warm, curious, natural American English.
Pace: 135 to 140 words per minute. Pause after each sentence.
Longer pause before "Right now?", before "So, what if", and before "Don't worry."
"Right now?" low and serious. The "So, what if" question rises at the end.

=== TEXT ===
No summer. No winter.

Right now?

Not yet.

So, what if Earth had no tilt?

Today, it leans twenty-three degrees.

That's the only reason you have seasons.

Straighten it up, and the Sun stays above the equator at noon. Every single day.

Day and night split twelve hours each. All year.

No blooming spring. No golden autumn.

The poles change the most. The Sun sits on the horizon, forever.

Barely any warmth. Ice keeps building.

And the monsoon? It follows the Sun north and south.

More than one billion people live under it.

Without that swing, it weakens.

Then comes the scary part.

Rain belts stop moving. Some places stay wet. Others stay dry. Forever.

Don't worry.

The Moon holds Earth's tilt steady, within about two and a half degrees.

Mars isn't so lucky. Its tilt swings by tens of degrees.

Follow to see how Earth really works.
