TITLE
No Summer. No Winter. 🌍 What If Earth Had No Tilt?

DESCRIPTION
What if Earth had no tilt? Today the planet leans about twenty-three degrees, and that lean is the only reason we have seasons.
Without it, the Sun would stay above the equator at noon every day, and day and night would split about twelve hours each all year.
At the poles the Sun would sit on the horizon all year, so they would get less warmth and ice would likely keep building.
The monsoon follows the Sun north and south, and more than one billion people live under it. A weaker swing would likely weaken it.
Earth's tilt stays steady because of the Moon, within about two and a half degrees (22.1 to 24.5 degrees over a 41,000-year cycle). Mars' tilt swings by tens of degrees.
Ice growth and monsoon changes are estimates.
Visuals are simplified and illustrative.
Map data: Natural Earth (public domain).

FACT CHECK
- Tilt 23.44 deg (23.4). OK.
- No tilt: Sun overhead at the equator at noon every day; day about 12 h everywhere (refraction adds minutes). OK.
- Poles without tilt: Sun circles at the horizon all year. OK. Ice growth: ESTIMATE (less annual polar sunlight).
- Monsoon population: more than 1 billion in the Indian monsoon region alone. OK. Weakening: ESTIMATE.
- Moon stabilises the tilt; 22.1 to 24.5 deg over about 41,000 years (range about 2.4). OK.
- Mars tilt varies strongly over millions of years (roughly 10 to 60 deg in simulations). "Tens of degrees" OK.
