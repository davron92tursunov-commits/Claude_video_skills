VOICE: Fola

=== STYLE INSTRUCTIONS ===
Read this as a documentary narrator: warm, curious, natural American English.
Pace: 135 to 140 words per minute. Pause after each sentence.
Longer pause before "Right now?", before "So, what if", and before "Don't worry."
"Right now?" low and serious. The "So, what if" question rises at the end.
Stress every number: three trillion, twenty-one percent, a quarter, one point six billion, twenty years.
"Then comes the scary part." slower and darker.
From "Don't worry." to the end: calm and reassuring.

=== TEXT ===
Every tree around you. Gone.

Right now?

Not yet.

So, what if every tree vanished?

Earth has three trillion of them.

Almost half are already gone.

Now, all the rest. Tonight.

But you wouldn't suffocate.

The air is twenty-one percent oxygen. It would last thousands of years.

The trouble is carbon.

Forests soak up about a quarter of the carbon dioxide we release.

Without them, it all stays in the sky.

Roots hold the ground together. Without them, hills slide, and rivers run brown.

Forests pump water into the air. Rain belts dry out.

And your city gets hotter, by several degrees.

Then comes the scary part.

One point six billion people depend on forests to live.

Don't worry.

Trees can't vanish overnight. And satellites show Earth has more green leaves than twenty years ago.

Follow to see how Earth really works.
